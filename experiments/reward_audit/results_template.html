Sparse SAC: running success-curve AUC

AUC integrates held-out simulator success over training transitions, using straight lines between evaluation checkpoints. The top curve divides the accumulated area by transitions elapsed, giving average success over training so far. It can decrease when later checkpoints perform worse. The bottom curve shows the accumulated area itself.

Individual curves include every recorded sparse run. The dark curve and band use only completed sparse seeds, independently of Robometer completion. Bands show ±1 sample SD across training seeds. Normalized AUC is undefined at zero transitions and starts at the first later evaluation.

Sparse reward through training

These plots include every recorded sparse-reward seed, independently of Robometer completion. The dark evaluation curve averages only completed sparse runs; its band is ±1 sample SD across those seeds. Partial runs are drawn individually and never treated as zeros.

With the native simulator reward, a failed episode returns 0 and a successful episode returns 1. Average episode return therefore equals the success fraction among completed training episodes in each window. Those stochastic training rollouts use states 0–24; held-out evaluation uses deterministic actions and states 25–49.

Training curves group episodes by the transition at which they finish, in 5,000-transition windows. Open circles mark an incomplete window. Unfinished episodes are excluded. The bottom panel counts terminal success rewards per 1,000 collected transitions, so it also depends on episode length. These are collected rewards, rather than rewards sampled from the SAC replay buffer. Evaluation transitions are excluded.

Decoder diagnostics

New reward workers always apply softmax to progress logits. An independent observer checks scores and records when the removed bypass would have triggered. The reference never supplies training rewards.

“Legacy condition” counts the old sum-equals-one test; corrected decoding never skips softmax. Counters are the latest persisted snapshot. Zero anomalies do not establish reward correctness.

Task and matched protocol

Simulator reward is the native sparse 0/1 signal. Robometer supplies progress in [0, 1] from the external camera and at most eight frames of the observed trajectory prefix. It receives no simulator-success bonus.

Both arms use simulator success for termination. SAC, DINO features, training budgets, and reset-state splits are held fixed; the policy and replay buffer start fresh for every seed. Robometer weights stay frozen.

How much evidence is this?

    Do not interpret three seeds × 25 evaluation episodes as 75 independent training seeds. The same held-out reset states are reused, and the learning algorithm is independently trained only once per seed and reward arm.
